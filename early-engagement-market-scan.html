Summary

What customers do in the first 90 days decides whether a bank becomes their main bank. Primacy is unlikely after day 90, and 50% of digitally opened accounts are gone by then. Leaders in every industry win the same way: they identify one early behaviour that predicts retention and put the whole onboarding behind it.

Paycheck first

Chime, Monzo, Revolut and Nubank link early pay, fee waivers and tiers to salary. Accounts with a direct deposit last about twice as long.

Card works on day 0

A virtual card plus wallet push provisioning gets 83% of cardholders paying within 3 days.

Borrow the habit layer

Duolingo: streaks. Streaming: taste questions. Telco: welcome calls. E-commerce: the second purchase. Vitality: rewards for repeated behaviour.

Six vendor layers

Engagement platforms, insight engines, lifecycle orchestration, salary switching, wallet provisioning and gamification.

Next step for a daily-banking PM: use cohort data to find our own "aha" threshold (for example salary + 5 card payments + 1 bill in 30 days), then build the day 0–90 journey to reach it.

Why the first 90 days decide the relationship

Every industry the scan covered finds the same pattern: what a customer does in the first 1–90 days predicts most of their lifetime value, and the chance to change it drops quickly after that.

Banking and fintech: what the leaders do in the first 90 days

The best-performing banks treat the paycheck as the main goal, and the first card payment as the habit that grows from it. Everything else in onboarding exists to make those two things happen early.

Patterns across these banks

  • Salary first, through an incentive the customer feels every month. Early pay, tiers and fee waivers are linked to salary, not one-off cash. Accounts with a direct deposit stay open about twice as long.
  • Spending from day 0. A virtual card plus wallet provisioning removes the 5–10 day wait for the plastic. Welcome rewards ask for a spend, not just an account opening.
  • A reason to open the app every payday. Pots, salary sorting and bill pots give the customer a job to do in the app when the salary arrives, which ties the salary, bill payments and app use together.
  • Bonuses alone don't create habits. The Financial Brand notes that sign-up bonuses are a top reason people hold cards that are now inactive (The Financial Brand, Nov 2026). Rewards work best when linked to a recurring behaviour.

Vendor landscape for early engagement

The market sells this capability in six layers. Most banks buy two or three of them and build the orchestration logic themselves.

1 · Engagement-banking platforms

Digital onboarding plus the journeys that follow (servicing, lending, daily banking), all in one front end.
Backbase (Forrester DBEP Leader, Q2 2026), Infosys Finacle, Temenos, Q2
One Backbase client grew from 2,000 to 21,000+ new customers a month (Backbase)

2 · Insight and nudge engines

Personalised insights from transaction data: bills due, salary arrived, savings nudges.
Personetics (often bundled through FIS), Meniga, Strands, Bud
iBank: 34.6% digital engagement and 31.4% click-through on insights. 74% of customers say they'd be more loyal to a bank that gives such tips (Personetics survey)

3 · Lifecycle and messaging orchestration

Triggered push, email, in-app and SMS journeys from behaviour events.
Braze, MoEngage, Airship, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, Adobe Journey Optimizer, Xtremepush, Infobip
Cross-industry tools. Airship's data shows finance apps leading day-30 activation (eMarketer)

4 · Salary switching

Moving salary and direct debits in the app.
Pinwheel, Atomic (also sold as Mastercard Deposit Switch), Argyle (US). In Europe: national switching services such as UK CASS and Austria's statutory account-switching process
Pinwheel reports 34% average conversion, up to 70%

5 · Card activation and wallet provisioning

Instant virtual card, push provisioning to Apple/Google/Samsung Pay.
Issuer processors (Fiserv, Thales, Idemia, Entrust) and BaaS issuers (Lithic, Stripe, Wise, Increase)
Up to 300% more wallet enrolment (TransFund)

6 · Gamification and rewards

Missions, progress bars, cashback for each "first" action.
StriveCloud, Talon.One, Cardlytics or other card-linked offers
Shine: 80% gamified flow completion vs. 15% baseline (Xtremepush/StriveCloud)

In-app guidance tools (Appcues, Pendo, Userpilot, WalkMe) and product analytics (Amplitude, Mixpanel) cover onboarding tours and finding activation thresholds. They are more common in SaaS than in banking apps.

A cross-industry playbook for daily banking

Put together, the evidence points to one journey: card in use on day 0, first payment in week 1, salary and bills moved by day 30, and a weekly habit by day 90. It borrows the best mechanic from each industry above.

Highlighted: the two milestones that most predict primacy. Read each lane from left to right. The four lanes run at the same time, and each phase passes on a single behaviour to the next.

Six principles that transfer across industries

  1. Find your own aha thresholdSocial media, SaaS
    Use cohort data to find which early combination predicts 12-month primacy. A likely candidate is salary + 5 card payments + 1 bill in 30 days. Then make the onboarding aim for that.
  2. Deliver value in the first sessionDuolingo, push provisioning
    Before the customer leaves the app on day 0 they should have a card in their phone wallet and one goal set.
  3. Reward behaviour that repeats, not sign-upChime tiers, Vitality
    Link benefits to monthly salary and card use. One-off cash brings in bonus hunters.
  4. Aim for the second action, not just the firstE-commerce
    The second card payment and the second bill payment show that a habit is forming.
  5. Get ahead of the first moment of frictionTelco
    Call or message in week 1. Explain the first statement and fees before they arrive.
  6. Personalise from a few questionsStreaming
    Two or three goal questions at sign-up decide which journey the customer gets.

Measurement beyond 30 days on book

  • Add day 7 (first card payment, wallet added) and day 90 (primacy: salary + bills + card habit). At day 30, many salaries haven't arrived yet and you can't see whether the habit lasts.
  • Track the share still active after the 6-month reward ends, to tell a real habit from activity the reward bought.
  • Use cohort analysis to test our aha threshold (e.g. salary + 5 card payments + 1 bill in 30 days) against 12-month primacy.
  • Keep a control group with no communications or rewards, so each pillar's real effect can be measured.

Next steps

  1. Find our own aha threshold: run a cohort analysis of 2024–2025 openings against 12-month primacy
  2. Baseline the KPIs above for the last 6 monthly cohorts
  3. Review the gaps on day 0: virtual card, push provisioning, salary question at sign-up
  4. Design a test that links the welcome reward to first spend plus salary, instead of to account opening
  5. Prototype a payday moment (salary sorter / bills pot) and a week-1 welcome contact
  6. Evaluate vendors for the gaps: insight engine, lifecycle orchestration, salary switching support
